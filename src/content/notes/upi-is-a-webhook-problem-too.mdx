I added UPI to [admit-one](/projects/admit-one/), the ticketing checkout I build in
public. My first plan was the one many checkouts still show: a box for the buyer's UPI
ID, and a "collect" request sent to their app. That plan is dead. NPCI ended collect for
payments to merchants on 28 February 2026, so a checkout asks the buyer's app to pay
instead. Building it that way taught me that UPI has the same problem as a card
redirect.

## Intent on a phone, a QR code on a computer

Instead of collecting, the shop hands the buyer a payment request to open. On a phone,
that's a `upi://pay` link that opens their UPI app. On a computer, it's the same link as
a QR code, scanned with the phone. The link carries everything the app needs:

```txt
upi://pay?pa=admit-one@example&pn=admit-one&mc=7922
  &tr=pay_1ee9d180ebdc&tn=admit-one%20order%20ord_3237dc52c99e
  &am=3038.50&cu=INR
```

Two of these parameters are easy to miss:

- **`mc` is the merchant category code** (7922 is ticket agencies). A payment to a
  merchant must carry it.
- **`tr` is a reference unique to this payment.** It's the payment's id, so when the
  confirmation comes back it matches exactly one payment.

The amount always has two decimals, worked out from integer paise without floats. The
payee, `admit-one@example`, belongs to no bank, so scanning the demo's code with a real
app can't pay anyone. I decoded the QR code from a screenshot to check it's exactly
that link.

## The approval happens somewhere you can't see

The buyer approves in their UPI app, between them and their bank. The checkout page
isn't part of that conversation, so it can't know the answer. If the page waited for
the app to report back, a closed app or a dead battery would leave it guessing.

That's the problem I'd already solved for cards: [the redirect races the
webhook](/notes/redirect-races-the-webhook/). The fix is the same. The page re-reads the
order every couple of seconds, and the payment provider's webhook decides. Approving
schedules `payment.succeeded`; declining, by the buyer or their bank, schedules
`payment.failed`. Both go through the same outbox and are deduplicated by event id.
Once the buyer approves, the checkout hands over to the order page, which waits for the
webhook exactly as it does for a card.

## A request has to end

A request that nobody approves can't wait forever:

- **It runs out after five minutes.** Like a ticket hold, that's worked out when it's
  read, not by a timer.
- **It never outlasts the hold.** If a buyer could approve after their seats had gone
  back on sale, the only honest outcome would be a refund.
- **It can be cancelled,** so the buyer can pay by card instead. Once they've approved,
  cancelling is refused: the money may already be moving.

## The small things

- **The QR code is black on white, even in dark mode.** Scanners read dark-on-light
  codes most reliably.
- **A phone gets a button, not a QR code.** You can't scan your own screen.
- **Focus follows what happened:** to the request when it appears, and to the notice
  when a payment is declined or a request runs out.

In the demo, a simulated phone appears below the checkout so you can approve or
decline. It talks to the sandbox, not to the shop's API, because that's where a real
answer happens too.

> A new payment method changes where the buyer says yes. It doesn't change who you
> believe.
